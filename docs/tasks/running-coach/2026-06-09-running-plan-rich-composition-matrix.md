## Original Problem

The deterministic preview engine could produce technically valid but runner-facing flat plans.
Easy, generic quality, and long-run shells repeated without enough goal meaning, while conservative
plans sometimes lost their training identity entirely.

This record introduced a detailed matrix for the former 10K, Half Marathon, and Marathon Base
families across runner levels and load contexts. It was accepted for that retired deterministic
engine and is retained so the original quality failure and its coaching lessons remain traceable.

## Durable Coaching Lessons

- Training variety must be purposeful rather than decorative.
- A plan should express the selected distance through its weekly rhythm, development sessions,
  long-run role, progression, cutbacks, taper, and endpoint.
- Conservative or low-availability plans may be simpler, but must not become long identity deserts.
- Beginner richness should come first from adaptation, support rhythm, strides where appropriate,
  gradual durability, and recovery rather than forced intensity.
- Supported runners may receive tempo, intervals, hills, threshold, progression, or long-run
  specificity only when those sessions serve the goal and recovery remains credible.
- A demanding midweek session and a demanding long-run finish must not be stacked merely to increase
  visible variety.
- The next running contact after a demanding session or long run should protect recovery.
- Cutbacks must visibly reduce load; taper must reduce fatigue without becoming empty filler.
- Long runs need a meaningful endurance purpose and believable progression, but must not carry all
  of a plan's richness alone.
- Exact selected-distance endpoint truth and non-medical, non-shaming language remain required.

These are coaching review principles, not fixed workout counts or a backend selection algorithm.

## Superseded Decisions

The following parts of the original matrix are no longer current instructions:

- deterministic backend plan authorship or hard-coded week composition;
- fixed runner-level and load-context workout-count tables;
- a live `Marathon Base` product family, endpoint, or fallback;
- mandatory old 10-week or 14-week family sequences;
- benchmark-only pace eligibility, rejection of accepted AI-authored estimated pace, or
  personal-only executable BPM;
- the rule that AI must not author the normal plan.

Current AI authoring may produce one accepted numeric pace or BPM command per runnable leaf under the
current provider/compiler and watch-target contracts. Backend validates and compiles that authored
truth without selecting, inserting, or replacing coaching content.
