# Universal No-Dead-End Doctrine - Historical Record

Date: 2026-06-10
Owner: Running Coach
Status: historical deterministic doctrine; bridge principle retained in current AI authoring

## Original Problem

The deterministic engine refused some ambitious goals solely because of runner level. The visible
example was a beginner Half Marathon request, even when a longer adaptation and durability bridge
could make the goal coach-plausible.

## Decision At The Time

The engine was instructed to auto-extend deterministic plans instead of blocking ambition, including
very long runner-level and family-specific horizon tables. It reserved unavailable states for
structural conflicts that additional time could not solve.

The fixed horizon tables, multi-year prescriptions, backend-owned phase composition, and Marathon
Base/Completion mappings are no longer current.

## Durable Lessons

- Runner level, ambitious distance, conservative load, body size, low current fitness, or a long
  required runway are not by themselves reasons to refuse or shame a runner.
- Add adaptation time when compression would make progression dishonest.
- Preserve explicit availability and fixed rest-day constraints.
- A plan may remain unavailable when valid workout placement, recovery, or the selected endpoint
  cannot be achieved inside the supported contract; do not hide that failure with filler.
- Explain a longer authored horizon as training context, without medical claims or asking the runner
  to choose an unsafe shorter version.

## Current Boundary

The retained principle is `extend rather than compress`, but AI owns the horizon, phases, and
progression inside the current provider/compiler limits. Backend may validate or reject the authored
draft; it must not independently stretch, shorten, repair, or replace the coaching plan.

The current provider contract also owns the novice first-session bridge: the first 14 calendar days
use only Run/Walk, Easy, or Recovery sessions, include at least four adaptation contacts with
recovery/rest between contacts, and place the first true Long Run no earlier than day 15.

Do not use this historical file to:

- revive the old 60-130 week tables;
- make backend author bridge phases;
- restore Marathon Base or Marathon Completion deterministic family routing;
- reject AI-authored estimated pace or accepted-profile BPM under old metric rules.
